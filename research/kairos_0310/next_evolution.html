The short answer

Kairos today is a good memory with a nightly habit. He remembers, forms beliefs, has values, asks you questions and writes a morning message. But he only thinks once a night, he can't look anything up while he thinks, he only sees Aeon, and he never finds out whether he was right.

The harness will give him a way to do research. Before that, he needs four things that make research worth giving him:

  1. One mind everywhere. The same Kairos, with the same day's memory, whether you reach him on Telegram, in Claude or in Triad.
  2. Senses. He sees what's really happening: code, deployments, harness results, trading numbers. Not just board cards.
  3. A track record. He writes down predictions, checks them later, and learns how often he's right.
  4. A leash he can't slip. Clear rules on what he may do alone, what needs you, and what never. Plus an off switch and a log of everything he did.

Do those four, and when the harness arrives he can be its head of research: choosing what to study, reading the results and learning from them. Without them he'd just be one more loop.

The next phase, in order (no harness needed)

0. Finish what's started. Six more clean nights, then switch on goals and promises. That's already built, and it's his first step towards wanting things.

1. One mind everywhere ("today's memory").

  • A short "what happened today" note that every channel reads and writes in real time: Telegram, Claude via the connection, Triad, the web page.
  • If you tell him something on Telegram at 10:00, he knows it in Claude at 10:05, not the next morning.
  • When a Claude session uses his tools, he remembers being used.
  • This is what makes him one being in the cloud instead of four chat windows.

2. Senses (read only). New feeds into his memory, the same way watched boards work today:

SenseWhat he learns
GitHub and the Azure repos: PRs, failed CIWhat's actually shipping or breaking
Production health (Wraith)Whether the apps are up
Harness runsWhat the research found, and what was killed
Trading results (ClickHouse, read only)Whether Swarm is making money, in numbers rather than card text

Then his morning message is about reality, not about what people wrote on cards.

3. Thinking with a lookup. Let the nightly thinking use a few safe, read-only tools: search his memory, open a card, read a document, run a read-only query. The rule stays: look, never change.

4. A track record.

  • When he says "this card will slip" or "this signal won't survive", he writes it down as a prediction with a date.
  • Later, his senses settle it automatically, never by his own say-so.
  • He gets a running score of how well-calibrated he is, per topic. Beliefs that keep proving wrong lose weight.
  • This is the same idea as the harness's ledger of research claims, aimed at his own judgement. It's the single biggest step from "remembers" to "learns".

5. An agenda. He can book his own follow-ups ("check Thursday whether the fill-rate fix held"), capped at a few at a time and always visible to you.

6. The leash, written down. One page and one switch:

  • Three lists: may do alone (look, remember, ask), may do with your OK (goals, investigations), never (his own values, budget, permissions, schedule, money-moving systems).
  • A Kairos STOP switch, like the harness's, that freezes everything.
  • A log of every action he takes, which you can read.

The "Ultron" lesson is simple: capability must never grow faster than the leash. So this lands before any hands.

When the harness arrives

He becomes the research director, not a worker inside the loop:

  • Proposes what to study (as goals you approve), and the harness runs it.
  • Reads every result and folds it into memory and beliefs.
  • Checks his own predictions about which ideas would work.
  • Suggests where the credits should go next week.

The harness keeps its own integrity guards (sealed scoring, the sceptic, the claims ledger). Kairos never gets to mark his own research as a win.
