# Recon: session claim ownership

Unique marker: `AEON_OS_E2E_2026-09-16T23-50-41-156Z-08668a_01`

Scope: `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts` and its focused claim tests in
`apps/web/src/lib/data/__tests__/sessions-claim.test.ts`. Repository research only; no

## Finding 1 — The claim predicate is a four-way filter evaluated inside a transaction with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`

**Label: Observed.**

`claimNextSession` opens a transaction at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:179` and
selects a candidate under a conjunction of four predicates: owner equality at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:184`, status equality to `'queued'` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:185`, a `task_id IS NOT NULL` requirement at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:186`, and engine membership at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:187`, all wrapped by the `and(` opened at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:183` and closed at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:188`. The candidate is ordered oldest-first by spawn
time at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:189` and locked with
`.for('update', { skipLocked: true })` at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:191`.

The ownership dimension is therefore the caller-supplied `userId`, not the worker
identity: a runner can only ever be handed a row whose `userId` column matches the
authenticated caller it polled as.

*Inference drawn from these lines:* the row-level lock plus `SKIP LOCKED` is the
mechanism by which two concurrently polling runners would select different candidate
rows rather than block on each other. The cited lines show the lock modifier; they do
not by themselves execute a concurrent scenario.

## Finding 2 — The claim is stamped onto the exact candidate row, inside the same transaction, before the row is returned

**Label: Observed.**

When no candidate matched, the function short-circuits at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:193` and returns `null`. Otherwise the same
transaction handle `tx` performs the update opened at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:196` against `agentSessions` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:197`, whose `set(` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:198` writes `status: 'running'` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:199`, `claimedBy: workerId` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:200` and `claimedAt: now` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:201`. The update is keyed to the previously locked
row by primary key at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:206`, and the flipped row is
returned at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:208`.

Ownership of a claim is thus two-part and written atomically with the status flip: the
persistent owner (`userId`, used to select) and the transient holder (`claimedBy`,
written here).

## Finding 3 — Heartbeats re-assert all three ownership facts, so a reassigned or finished claim goes silent

**Label: Observed.**

`heartbeatSession` is declared with an `(id, userId, workerId)` signature at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:216`. Its update, opened at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:218` against `agentSessions` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:219`, writes only the two timestamp fields at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:220`. The `and(` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:221` combines four predicates: session id at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:222`, owning user at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:223`, `claimedBy` equal to the pinging worker at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:224`, and status still `'running'` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:225`. The affected row is read back via
`.returning()` at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:227`.

*Inference drawn from these lines:* because the worker id from Finding 2 is re-checked
here, a row whose `claimedBy` was overwritten, or whose status left `'running'`, yields
no returned row — which is how the caller can distinguish a live claim from a lost one.
The cited lines show the predicate and the `returning()`; the caller's interpretation of
an empty result is not carried by these lines.

## Finding 5 — Terminal-result recording is guarded in the WHERE clause, not by a prior read

**Label: Observed.**

The settle update's `and(` at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:284` pairs the session
id at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:285` with
`notInArray(agentSessions.status, TERMINAL_STATUSES)` at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:286`. The updated row is read back at
`apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:288`, and an empty result returns early with
`guarded: true` at `apps/web/src/lib/data/sessions.ts:289`, before any card write.

*Inference drawn from these lines:* since the terminal-status exclusion is part of the
statement's own predicate rather than a separate read, two result posts arriving for the
same session would be serialised by the database and only one would receive a row. The
cited lines show the predicate and the early return; they do not execute a race.
